# U-SPACE Workshop 2027

This repository contains the website for the third U-SPACE Workshop. It is a 2027 update of the public 2026 workshop site and repository:

- <https://uspace-workshop.github.io/2026/>
- <https://github.com/uspace-workshop/2026>

Dates, venue and location are intentionally left as **TBC**.

## About the workshop

U-SPACE Workshop 2027 concerns the dependable integration of UAVs into low-altitude urban airspace. It is aimed at researchers and practitioners working across dependable computing, autonomy, aviation, communications, cybersecurity, transport, environmental science, public health and human factors.

The central question is practical: what evidence and engineering are needed before an urban UAV capability can be used responsibly? This includes the aircraft, but also the airspace services, communications, telemetry, software, people, organisations and rules around it.

The workshop should be of interest to projects and researchers working within SESAR and the Digital European Sky, UKRI programmes, and Horizon Europe. We welcome work that can contribute to research agendas, demonstrations, field trials, safety cases, standards, regulation, procurement or later deployment. We also welcome negative results and careful accounts of unresolved problems.

Urban UAVs may support infrastructure inspection, emergency response, environmental monitoring, scientific observation, accessibility and other useful services. They can also produce noise, emissions, ecological disturbance, safety risks, health impacts and intrusive forms of observation. The workshop therefore treats technical performance, public value and the limits of a proposed use as connected questions.

## Areas of interest

- Safety, dependability and resilience of UAV and U-space systems.
- Safety cases, verification, validation and assurance for autonomous or AI-enabled functions.
- Uncertainty in sensing, navigation, prediction and decision-making.
- U-space services, UTM, telemetry, command and control, identity and interoperability.
- GNSS resilience, communications, cybersecurity and protection against misuse.
- Fleet operations, traffic management and beyond-visual-line-of-sight operations.
- Testbeds, digital twins, field trials, incident learning and deployment evidence.
- Human factors, remote piloting, supervision, workload and accountability.
- Noise, emissions, energy use, lifecycle impacts, biodiversity and climate resilience.
- Public health, occupational safety, accessibility and inclusive service design.
- Privacy-preserving sensing, data minimisation, proportionality and civil liberties.
- Ethical governance, transparency, redress, public participation and dual-use concerns.
- Policy, regulation, standardisation, procurement and routes to adoption.

## Expectations for submissions

Submissions should make the research or operational problem clear. Where appropriate, authors should describe:

1. The intended operation, users and affected people or communities.
2. The system boundary, assumptions, dependencies and operating conditions.
3. The evidence used, including validation conditions and uncertainty.
4. Failure modes, degraded operation, human responsibilities and accountability.
5. Relevant security, privacy, ethical, health, environmental and equity issues.
6. The next step: further research, testing, assurance, standardisation, policy or operation.

Technical novelty is welcome, but a new method is not by itself an operational argument. Claims should be proportionate to the evidence. If a proposal involves cameras, microphones, persistent location data, behavioural inference or other personal data, it should explain why that data is necessary, what less intrusive alternatives were considered, and how collection, access, retention and reuse are controlled.

## Format and website

The site is a static HTML/CSS/JavaScript website with the following pages:

- `index.html` — workshop overview and topics.
- `call-for-papers.html` — scope and suggested topics.
- `program.html` — provisional program structure.
- `organization.html` — chairs and committee placeholders.
- `submission.html` — submission guidance and placeholders.
- `assets/css/styles.css` — shared styling.
- `script.js` — small-screen navigation.
- `assets/uspace2027-banner.png` — text-free banner graphic derived from the supplied U-SPACE logo palette and motifs.
- `assets/uspace2027-subpage-banner.png` — shallow full-width banner used below the navigation on interior pages.

The presentation deliberately stays close to the original conference-site style: text first, limited decoration, conventional navigation and no claims about confirmed arrangements. The banner uses the logo's deep navy (`#0B1E3B`), cyan (`#4FC3F7`), blue (`#1E88E5`) and yellow (`#FFCA28`) as accents. All dates, venue, location, contacts, submission system and publication information remain **TBC** until agreed by the organisers.
